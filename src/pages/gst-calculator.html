GST rates after GST 2.0 (from 22 September 2025)

GST was simplified into two main slabs, 5% and 18%, plus a special 40% rate for luxury and "sin" goods. The old 12% and 28% slabs were removed.

RateExamples
0%Fresh milk, paneer, bread, most fresh food, health and life insurance premiums
5%Packaged food, soaps and shampoo, many medicines, small hotels, restaurants
18%Most goods and services: electronics, ACs, small cars, two-wheelers up to 350cc, professional services
40%Large cars, motorcycles over 350cc, aerated and sugary drinks, tobacco products
3% / 0.25%Gold and silver jewellery / rough diamonds

Rates are set item by item. Check the exact rate for your HSN/SAC code on the CBIC website before invoicing.

GST formulas

Adding GST:

GST = Price × Rate ÷ 100
Total = Price + GST

Removing GST from an inclusive price:

Price before GST = Total × 100 ÷ (100 + Rate)
GST = Total − Price before GST

Example: a ₹1,180 bill with 18% GST included has a base price of ₹1,180 × 100 ÷ 118 = ₹1,000, and ₹180 GST.

CGST, SGST and IGST

  • Selling within your state: GST is split equally into CGST (central) and SGST (state). 18% becomes 9% + 9%.
  • Selling to another state: the full amount is IGST.

Frequently asked questions

Is there still a 12% or 28% GST slab?

No. From 22 September 2025, most items in the 12% slab moved to 5%, and most items in the 28% slab moved to 18%. Luxury and sin goods now attract 40%. Use the custom rate box for any old invoice.

How do I calculate GST from the total amount?

Choose "Price with GST" above and enter the total. The calculator divides it by (1 + rate) to find the base price, and the difference is the GST.

Who needs to register for GST?

Businesses selling goods with yearly turnover above ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh in some states), or services above ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in some states), must register. Some businesses, like online sellers, must register regardless of turnover.
